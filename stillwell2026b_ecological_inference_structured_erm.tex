\begin{abstract}%
Geographic models often fit latitude or climate strongly in sample, then
fail at withheld sites---a pattern now routine in continental mapping
studies.  We show that the failure is estimand mismatch, not
model pathology: nested surveys are empirical risk minimization on
\emph{structured} data, and certifying within-site interpolation as
across-site transfer optimizes the wrong functional.  Ecological inference
and domain-generalization learning are therefore one geometry---ERM under
nested units, confounded gradients, and niche support---the spatial
counterpart to temporal learning in a companion preprint on selection as
ERM across generations.  Semi-synthetic simulation (500 replicates; ESM)
yields 84\% disagreement between strongest in-sample $|r|$ and LOPO ranking
under a stated generating process---a design-specific rate, not an
ecological law.  A published beetle cline (\emph{Stator limbatus};
$n=93$) exhibits the same qualitative split: multivariate ecology wins on
holdout (LOPO RMSE 0.104 vs.\ 0.112) while proxy correlates dominate in
sample.  The practical rule is explicit: claims about \emph{new sites}
require population-level holdout (\Etwo), not individual $k$-fold CV.
Proofs and supplementary tables are in the ESM.
\end{abstract}

\begin{keywords}
  empirical risk minimization, cross-validation, domain generalization,
  species distribution models, macroecology, niche theory, Bergmann's rule
\end{keywords}

\section*{Media summary}
Climate and biogeographic models often fit strongly across surveyed sites yet
fail at new locations---a costly error for range-shift and conservation
planning.  The fault is rarely the correlate and often the certificate:
in-sample fit can validate the wrong target.  Under a stated semi-synthetic
design, the strongest in-sample correlate and leave-one-population-out
validation disagree on model ranking in 84\% of replicates; on a beetle
survey across western North America, individual $k$-fold cross-validation
inflated error by ${\sim}$45\% relative to population holdout.  Match the
holdout unit to the claim---withhold whole populations or regions when the
goal is prediction at new sites---and treat the problem as structured
generalization: the spatial face of the same geometry that governs learning
across generations in evolution and in machine learning.

\section{Introduction: Geography and the Risk Functional}
\label{sec:intro}
% ==================================================================

Macroecologists and species-distribution modelers inherit a familiar
pattern: excellent in-sample fit to latitude or climate, then poor performance
at withheld sites, under range shifts, or in regional transfer
\citep{araujo2006,ploton2020}.  The paradox dissolves once the estimand is
named.  The field is not short of correlates; it is short of explicit
targets---\emph{what exactly} must a cline or distribution model predict?
When the claim is a new population mean at a new coordinate, high in-sample
$R^2$ can certify the wrong question.

That ambiguity is quantitative, not rhetorical.  Every cline analysis and
species-distribution model minimizes some empirical risk; validity hinges on
whether that functional matches the estimand.  Until it does, proxy correlates
along smooth geographic gradients will read as mechanisms, and holdout schemes
that leak site identity will inflate apparent accuracy
\citep{roberts2017,ploton2020}.

The same mathematics spans two literatures that have rarely met at this
abstraction.  Macroecology, niche theory, and spatial statistics specify
\emph{what} is predicted across sites
\citep{grinnell1917,hutchinson1957,roberts2017}; domain generalization,
grouped cross-validation, and distribution-shift theory specify \emph{how}
holdout geometry must respect group structure under transfer
\citep{koh2021,quinonero2009,arjovsky2019}.  A companion preprint
\citep{stillwell2026a} treats natural selection as empirical risk
minimization across \emph{generations} (Volume~I).  Here we treat
\emph{space} as the orthogonal axis: the unit of generalization, geographic
confounding, and the niche envelope jointly define holdout risk.  The
correspondence in Table~\ref{tab:correspondence} is self-contained; acceptance
of Volume~I is not presupposed.

\paragraph{For readers from ecology.}
Individual $k$-fold CV certifies within-population interpolation (\Eone);
cline and SDM claims require across-site transfer (\Etwo).  Latitude and
temperature on smooth gradients are structured confounders, not innocent
covariates; Grinnellian niche extent is training support; realized-niche
context is hidden structure that can shift labels under transfer.

\paragraph{For readers from machine learning.}
Geographic surveys are nested domain-generalization problems: populations
are groups, clines are confounded manifolds, and LOPO or spatial block CV
are the honest estimators for population-level claims.  Field clines analyzed
without common-garden decomposition minimize composite-label risk.

Roberts et al.\ and successors prescribe \emph{how} to hold out spatially
\citep{roberts2017,ploton2020}; what has been missing is a unified ERM
account that links clines, SDMs, plasticity bias, and interaction tests under
one geometry.  Volume~I closes Valiant's evolvability gap in
quantitative-genetic coordinates; Volume~II closes the parallel gap for
spatial generalization in ecological coordinates
(Table~\ref{tab:correspondence}).  We establish estimand--CV mismatch
(Theorem~\ref{thm:estimand-cv-mismatch}) and proxy rank reversal under
gradient confounding (Theorem~\ref{thm:rank-reversal}); extend the framework
to support, hidden context, and plasticity (Propositions~\ref{prop:support}--\ref{prop:plasticity}; proofs in the ESM); separate observational
generalization from mechanistic certification
(Theorems~\ref{thm:mechanism}--\ref{thm:two-test}); and stress-test the
logic in simulation and one published cline vignette
(Figures~\ref{fig:sim-mismatch}--\ref{fig:empirical-summary}).

\subsection{The Formal Correspondence}
\label{sec:correspondence}

At root, every geographic model is an empirical risk minimization problem on
nested data: individuals within sites, sites along gradients, gradients
confounded with mechanism.  Table~\ref{tab:correspondence} makes that
structure explicit---the spatial translation map for ecological inference
and structured machine learning, analogous to the temporal correspondence in
Volume~I \citep{stillwell2026a}.

  \caption{Correspondence between ecological inference and structured machine
    learning.  Volume~I mapped temporal learning; this table maps spatial
    generalization under nested sampling, confounding, and support constraints.}
  \label{tab:correspondence}
  \begin{center}
  \small
  \begin{tabular}{p{0.44\linewidth}p{0.44\linewidth}}
    \toprule

    Individual within known site & i.i.d.\ test draw \\
    Macroecological / cline claim & Domain-generalization estimand \\
    Leave-one-population-out (LOPO) & Leave-one-group-out CV \\

    Geographic gradient $g$ & Index of confounded manifold \\
    Latitude / temperature proxy & Spurious correlate; shortcut feature \\
    Multivariate ecology model & Structural / causal feature set \\
    Grinnellian niche envelope & Training support $\supp(P_{\mathbf{x}})$ \\
    Realized niche / biotic context & Hidden context; label shift \\
    Field cline (phenotype) & Composite label (genetic + plastic) \\
    Common-garden slope & Correct supervision target \\
    Range limit & Support boundary; OOD failure \\
    Env $\times$ trait interaction test & Mechanism certification \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
  \end{center}
\end{table}

Data are nested: individual $i$ in population $p$, site covariates
$\mathbf{x}_p$, response $y_{ip}$ or mean $\bar{y}_p$, with
\begin{equation}
  y_{ip} = \mu(\mathbf{x}_p) + u_p + \varepsilon_{ip},
  \qquad \mathbb{E}[u_p \mid \mathbf{x}_p] = 0 .
  \label{eq:nested-generative}
\end{equation}
Macroecological hypotheses concern $\mu(\cdot)$---the predictable component
at the site level---not within-population noise $\varepsilon_{ip}$.

\subsection{Formal Results}
\label{sec:formal-results}

The formal results below are failure-mode theorems: each names a way
structured sampling geometry breaks inference when ignored (full proofs,
ESM Sections~2--8).

\begin{definition}[Three estimands]
\label{def:three-estimands}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \Eone\ (within-site): new individuals at known populations.
  \item \Etwo\ (across-site): new population means at new coordinates.
  \item \Ethree\ (across-environment): new regions of niche space
        (extrapolation / OOD).
\end{enumerate}
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Estimand--CV mismatch]
\label{thm:estimand-cv-mismatch}
Let $\Rkfold(h)$ denote $K$-fold CV over individuals and $\RLOPO(h)$
LOPO on population means.  When $\mathrm{Var}(\varepsilon_{ip}\mid
p)>0$ and the estimand is \Etwo, $\mathbb{E}[\Rkfold(h)] \neq
\mathbb{E}[\RLOPO(h)]$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
For squared loss, risk at a new site mean decomposes as
$R_2(h)=\mathbb{E}[(h(\mathbf{x}_{p^\star})-\mu(\mathbf{x}_{p^\star}))^2]
+\mathrm{Var}(\bar{u}_{p^\star})$.  At a known site, a new individual
contributes an extra irreducible term:
$\mathbb{E}[(h(\mathbf{x}_p)-y_{i^\star p})^2\mid p]
=(h(\mathbf{x}_p)-\mu(\mathbf{x}_p))^2+\mathrm{Var}(\varepsilon_{ip}\mid p)
+\cdots$.  Hence $R_1(h)=R_2(h)+\mathbb{E}[\mathrm{Var}(\varepsilon_{ip}\mid p)]$
whenever test individuals share training-site identity---within-population
noise enters $R_1$ but not population-mean prediction at a new site
$p^\star$.  Individual $k$-fold CV averages that term into its target;
LOPO on $\bar{y}_p$ does not.  Full derivation: ESM, Section~2.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Operational default for geographic claims]
\label{cor:operational-default}
\textbf{Default.}  If the scientific claim is a \emph{new population mean} at
a \emph{new site}, the estimand is \Etwo: holdout must partition at the
population or spatial-block level (LOPO, leave-one-group-out, or block CV
\citep{roberts2017})---not individual $k$-fold CV.  Name the estimand
before interpreting fit.
\end{corollary}

\begin{lemma}[Oracle risk ordering on true support]
\label{lem:oracle}
Under \eqref{eq:nested-generative} with linear $\mu(\mathbf{x})=
\mathbf{x}^\top\boldsymbol{\beta}^\star$, population-level OLS on the
true support $\mathcal{S}^\star=\{k:\beta_k^\star\neq 0\}$ minimizes
squared prediction risk among predictors using only
$\mathbf{x}_{\mathcal{S}^\star}$.  This follows from standard OLS
optimality \citep{hastie2009} and does \emph{not}, by itself, explain
in-sample proxy rankings.
\end{lemma}

\begin{theorem}[Proxy rank reversal]
\label{thm:rank-reversal}
Along $\mathbf{x}_p = \boldsymbol{\phi}(g_p)+\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_p$ with
$y_p = \mathbf{x}_p^\top\boldsymbol{\beta}^\star+\eta_p$, suppose coordinate
$x_j$ is a non-sufficient proxy ($\beta_j^\star=0$ but
$\mathrm{Corr}(x_j,\mathbf{x}_{\mathcal{S}^\star})\neq 0$).  Then
in-sample $|r|$ or AIC can rank $h_j$ above the structural model on
$\mathcal{S}^\star$ while LOPO ranks them in reverse order
\citep{burnham2002,geirhos2020,dormann2013}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
In-sample univariate slope on $x_j$ absorbs covariance with the true drivers
$\mathbf{x}_{\mathcal{S}^\star}$ along the gradient.  At a held-out site $p$
with local deviation from the bivariate collinearity structure, univariate
extrapolation error grows while the multivariate model retains
$\mathbf{x}_{p,\mathcal{S}^\star}$.  Constructive argument and simulation:
ESM, Section~4.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Grinnellian support bound]
\label{prop:support}
Training environments $\mathbf{x}_p\in\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{train}}$;
for Lipschitz $h$, extrapolation error at
$\mathbf{x}\notin\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{train}}$ grows with
$\mathrm{dist}(\mathbf{x},\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{train}})$ unless invariance
is assumed \citep{grinnell1917,ploton2020,araujo2019}.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
Triangle inequality on $|h(\mathbf{x})-h(\mathbf{x}_0)|$ for nearest
$\mathbf{x}_0\in\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{train}}$; range limits are threshold
crossings outside observed support.  ESM, Section~5.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Realized niche as hidden context]
\label{prop:hidden-context}
If biotic context $b_p$ enters $y_p=f(\mathbf{x}_p,b_p)+\eta_p$ but models
use only $\mathbf{x}_p$, then transfer where $P(b\mid\mathbf{x})$ shifts
induces label shift at fixed $\mathbf{x}$ \citep{soberon2007,koh2021}.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
Marginal target is $\mathbb{E}_{b\mid\mathbf{x}}[f(\mathbf{x},b)]$; a shift in
$P(b\mid\mathbf{x})$ changes the composite label without moving $\mathbf{x}$.
ESM, Section~6.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Plasticity as composite-label risk]
\label{prop:plasticity}
Field clines $y^{\mathrm{field}}_{p}=g^{\mathrm{gen}}_{p}+g^{\mathrm{plast}}
(\mathbf{x}_p)+\epsilon_{p}$ make ERM on phenotypes target a genetic--plastic
sum, not $g^{\mathrm{gen}}$ alone \citep{via1985,stillwell2010}.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
Bayes-optimal $h^\star(\mathbf{x})=g^{\mathrm{gen}}(\mathbf{x})+
g^{\mathrm{plast}}(\mathbf{x})$ under squared loss; genetic slopes require
common-garden supervision.  ESM, Section~7.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Mechanistic adequacy]
\label{thm:mechanism}
A hypothesis that environment modulates selection on trait $z$ implies a
$z \times e$ interaction in any adequate fitness model; additive
correlational models can fit clines in sample yet fail mechanistic tests
under manipulation or cross-environment holdout \citep{lande1983,stillwell2008}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Two-test logic]
\label{thm:two-test}
Mechanism certification requires (i)~structured holdout (\Etwo) and
(ii)~the required interaction under manipulation; neither alone suffices.
\end{theorem}

\subsection{Empirical illustration}
\label{sec:empirical-validation}

\paragraph{Semi-synthetic clines.}
To isolate gradient confounding under known truth, we simulated 500 surveys
of $n=95$ populations each \citep{dormann2013}: ecology drove trait PC1
while latitude remained a correlated proxy (ESM simulation protocol).  Under
this design, latitude had the strongest in-sample $|r|$ in \textbf{100\%} of
replicates; the ecology multivariate model minimized LOPO RMSE in
\textbf{84\%}; the two criteria disagreed in \textbf{84\%}
(Figure~\ref{fig:sim-mismatch}).  The rates are properties of the stated
parameterization, not ecology at large.

\paragraph{Scope.}
The simulation asks whether a confounded gradient produces the predicted
rank reversal; the \emph{Stator} reanalysis asks whether a published cline
shows the same qualitative split---conspicuous proxy $|r|$, structural model
preferred on holdout.  Neither replaces a multi-species meta-analysis
\citep{ploton2020,roberts2017}.

  \begin{center}
  \includegraphics[width=0.72\linewidth]{fig1_simulation_lopo_mismatch_rates.png}
  \end{center}
  \caption{Semi-synthetic stress test ($500$ replicates).  Under a stated
    ecology-driven generating process, the strongest in-sample univariate
    correlate (latitude) mis-ranks models relative to LOPO in 84\% of
    replicates---proxy confounding made quantitative.}
  \label{fig:sim-mismatch}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{Geographic cline reanalysis.}
On \emph{Stator limbatus} \citep{stillwell2007} ($n=93$), ecology achieved
lowest LOPO RMSE (\textbf{0.104}) versus temperature (\textbf{0.112}; 7.7\%
higher); latitude led in-sample ($|r|=0.416$) but not on holdout (0.106;
ESM Table~S1).  Individual 10-fold CV inflated ecology RMSE by ${\sim}$45\%
(0.150 vs.\ 0.104; ESM Table~S4;
Theorem~\ref{thm:estimand-cv-mismatch})---the estimand error made visible.
A paired selection experiment \citep{stillwell2008} rejected the required
large-line $\times$ low-temperature interaction ($p = 0.017$;
Theorem~\ref{thm:two-test}): observational generalization and manipulative
mechanism failed on different criteria, as the two-test theorem requires.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}
% ==================================================================

Ecological inference is structured empirical risk minimization: the question
is not whether models fit, but which risk functional their validation
certifies.  Volume~I formalizes \emph{update} across generations;
Volume~II formalizes \emph{transfer} across space---one geometry, two axes.
Corollary~\ref{cor:operational-default} is the rule practitioners can apply
tomorrow: name the estimand, then match the holdout unit, before reading
Bergmann clines, SDMs, or climate-correlate narratives as mechanism
\citep{blackburn1999,shelomi2012,ploton2020}.  When functional and estimand
misalign, in-sample fit overstates transfer and proxy correlates defeat
structural models on the metric that matters.

For machine learning, geographic data are grouped domain-generalization
problems in the wild \citep{koh2021,arjovsky2019}---with population-level
estimands, composite labels from plasticity, and manipulative mechanism tests
that textbook benchmarks rarely encode.  Readers of the two-volume program
may read Volume~I as a coordinate transform: local adaptation as subdomain
fine-tuning, range limits as support-boundary failure, plasticity as
misspecified supervision \citep{stillwell2026a}; none of that is required
to accept the spatial claims developed here.

Structured ERM is deliberately about predictive geometry, not conservation
ethics or full causal discovery \citep{pearl2009}.  Open work includes
optimal spatial block size, invariance under confounded gradients, and
replication across taxa \citep{roberts2017,ploton2020}.  The contribution is
a vocabulary and a default: treat every geographic claim as a grouped
generalization problem, and validate it as such.

\section{Methods}
\label{sec:methods}
% ==================================================================

We generated 500 semi-synthetic surveys of $n=95$ populations.  Ecology
predictors drove trait PC1 while latitude served as a correlated proxy
(ESM simulation protocol).  LOPO used leave-one-out ordinary least squares on
standardized population-level predictors, following structured holdout
practice for grouped data \citep{roberts2017,varoquaux2018}.  Population means
and bootstrap paired comparisons for \emph{S.\ limbatus} follow Stillwell
et al.\ \citep{stillwell2007}; protocol details are in ESM Tables S1--S4.
All scripts reproduce Figures~1--2 and Table~\ref{tab:stator-lopo}:
\repoUrl{} (see \texttt{REPRODUCE.md}).  A permanent Zenodo archive will be
minted at submission.
