# Cover letter — Volume II (Interface companion submission)

**Manuscript:** *Ecological Inference Is Structured Empirical Risk Minimization: Generalization Across Space, Nested Units, and Niche Support*  
**Author:** R. Craig Stillwell  
**Companion:** Volume I — *Natural Selection Is Empirical Risk Minimization* (submitted to *Journal of the Royal Society Interface*, Project 42)

---

Dear Editor,

I am preparing **Volume II** of a two-part theory program on empirical risk minimization (ERM) in biology. Volume I (Stillwell, 2026a), currently under consideration at *Interface*, establishes that natural selection is ERM on a Fisher-information manifold across **generations**. Volume II completes the program for **ecology**: when does a fitted mapping from environment to trait or distribution generalize across space, nested populations, and niche support?

Ecologists and macroecologists often rank in-sample correlates (latitude, temperature) as if they certify prediction at unseen sites. Machine learning has formal tools for structured holdout and domain shift, but ecology lacks a unified framework. Volume II supplies that framework: nine linked propositions (proofs in electronic supplementary material), semi-synthetic simulation showing rank reversal between in-sample correlation and leave-one-population-out validation, and a short vignette from a published geographic cline (*Stator limbatus*).

**Why Interface for Volume II:** Volume I is submitted here as a cross-disciplinary bridge between evolutionary genetics and learning theory. Volume II is the spatial complement—same ERM geometry, different axis (geography vs generations) and failure mode (confounding vs drift). Keeping both volumes in *Interface* preserves a coherent two-paper program for readers at the biology–computation interface. A separate methods companion on LOPO workflow (Project 50) will cite Volume II but is not a substitute for the theory.

**Suggested reviewers:** macroecology and species distribution models; spatial cross-validation; phenotypic plasticity; domain generalization / distribution shift.

Volume I manuscript is available on request.
