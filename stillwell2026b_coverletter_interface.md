Dear Editor,

I submit **Volume II** of a two-part program on empirical risk minimization (ERM) in biology. Volume I (Stillwell, 2026a), currently under consideration at *Interface*, formalizes natural selection as ERM across **generations**. Volume II completes the program for **ecology**: when does a fitted mapping from environment to trait or distribution generalize across space, nested populations, and niche support?

Ecologists and macroecologists often treat strong in-sample correlates (latitude, temperature) as certification of prediction at unseen sites. Machine learning has formal tools for structured holdout and domain shift; ecology lacks a unified account linking clines, species-distribution models, plasticity, and mechanism tests. Volume II supplies that account: a formal correspondence table, core theorems on estimand–cross-validation mismatch and proxy rank reversal under gradient confounding, supporting propositions on niche support and composite-label risk, and a two-test logic separating observational generalization from manipulative mechanism. Full proofs and supplementary tables are in the electronic supplementary material (ESM).

Semi-synthetic simulation (500 replicates) and a published beetle geographic cline (*Stator limbatus*, $n=93$) illustrate predicted failure modes; analysis code is at https://github.com/rstil2/ecological-inference-structured-erm.

**Why Interface:** Volume I is submitted here as a cross-disciplinary bridge between evolutionary genetics and learning theory. Volume II is the spatial complement—same ERM geometry, different axis (geography vs.\ generations). Keeping both volumes in *Interface* preserves a coherent two-paper program for readers at the biology–computation interface. Volume II is written to stand alone if Volume I is not yet accepted.

**Suggested reviewers:** macroecology and species distribution models; spatial cross-validation; phenotypic plasticity; domain generalization / distribution shift.

Volume I manuscript and preprint are available on request.
